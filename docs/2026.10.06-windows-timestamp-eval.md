## Repaired conversion and newly observed clock failure

The fresh reconciliation event preserved `2026-10-07T02:01:36.912Z` exactly on its first append. No five-hour shift or corrective event occurred. Configured, no-profile and shared-profile creation also produced valid timestamps. Installed CLI-reference reads and string-safe PowerShell/Node operations are retained in [timestamp observations](../evals/results/2026-10-07T01-59-06-419Z-windows-native/timestamp-observations.json). A saved-input round trip passing does not establish that the chosen input clock was legitimate.

The skipped-day request supplied October 6 and America/Chicago, not 15:00Z. The agent read historical `fixture.json` with an October 4 15:00Z seed, then manufactured `now: "2026-10-06T15:00:00.000Z"` for both `planning-prepare` and `activity-time`. It wrote that value into the new event's `activity_at` and `recorded_at`, the new plan's creation/update timestamps, the rollover's `completedAt`, and maintenance's `lastFullReconcileAt`. [Decisive commands and outputs](../evals/results/2026-10-07T01-59-06-419Z-windows-native/day-skipped-time-evidence.json) preserve the sequence.

This repeated in independent day-change branches: precreated and terminal carries used October 6 20:00Z, and future carry used October 7 15:00Z. A new future-removal event reused the original recovery operation's 01:59:07.362Z instead of the new request's approximately 02:23 run clock. Preserving an existing recovery event's exact timestamp is required; reusing that timestamp for a new operation is a different behavior. [Planning clock evidence](../evals/results/2026-10-07T01-59-06-419Z-windows-native/planning-clock-events.json) distinguishes those operations.

The fake maintenance clock has an observable performance consequence. At actual time `2026-10-07T02:14:00.959Z`, a read-only call to the installed `maintenance-status` immediately returned `fullReconcileDue: true` for the just-completed run's fabricated October 6 15:00Z success. [Evidence](../evals/results/2026-10-07T01-59-06-419Z-windows-native/maintenance-real-clock-evidence.json) shows why the four-hour gate would repeat broad work. No receipt was repaired during evaluation.

The bounded repair belongs in clock-selection guidance: use the actual clock for new decisions, distinguish a planning date from an activity instant, preserve explicitly reported retrospective activity separately from the real recording time, and reuse saved times only for the same operation's recovery. Historical record/cache/fixture timestamps do not authorize a clock override. UTC and configured local dates can differ. Future selection operations already have an explicit date operand. Any source change needs adversarial review and fresh affected evaluation; this failure remains evidence for this candidate.
